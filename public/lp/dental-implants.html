Paying for treatment

How our patients manage the cost

Implant treatment is a significant investment, and almost nobody pays for a full arch out of a savings account. Here is how it is usually done.

Payment plans

Spread the cost across manageable instalments rather than paying up front. Our patient coordinator will walk you through the plans available and what the repayments actually look like for your case. Approval is subject to the provider's criteria.

Early release of super

Many patients access part of their superannuation early on compassionate grounds to fund major dental treatment. Applications are assessed by the ATO, and our team provides the clinical documentation to support yours. Eligibility is decided by the ATO, not by us.

Health fund rebates

Extras cover often contributes toward surgical fees, and if your surgery is under a general anaesthetic in hospital, your hospital cover may apply to theatre and anaesthetist costs. We give you the item numbers so you can confirm your exact rebate before you commit.

A written quote, every time

You receive an itemised treatment plan after your consultation covering the surgical fee, and where relevant the hospital and anaesthetist fees billed separately. You know the full figure before you decide anything.

About dental implants

From one tooth to a full arch

The right option depends on how many teeth are missing and how much bone is left to work with. Your consultation and 3D scan settle it.

A single missing tooth

One implant replaces the root and a crown sits on top, so nothing is ground down on the teeth either side and the bone that would otherwise shrink is preserved. Where a tooth is failing, it can often be removed and the implant placed at the same visit.

Several teeth, or a loose denture

A small number of implants can carry a bridge across a gap, or secure a denture that has never sat comfortably. It stops the rocking and the adhesive and restores the ability to eat properly, without replacing every tooth in the arch.

A full arch, All-on-4

Every tooth in the upper or lower jaw replaced on four or more implants, fixed to the jaw rather than resting on the gum. In most cases a temporary bridge is fitted on the day of surgery, and most patients do not need bone grafting.

A single implant

A replacement root, not just a replacement tooth.

A dental implant is a titanium fixture placed into the jaw to take the place of a missing tooth root. Once the bone has grown around it, a crown made by your dentist or prosthodontist is secured on top.

Unlike a bridge it does not rely on cutting down the healthy teeth on either side, and unlike a denture it does not move. It also preserves the jaw bone, which starts to shrink as soon as a tooth is lost.

  • Immediate implants. Where a tooth is failing, it can often be removed and the implant placed at the same appointment.
  • Decades of service with good hygiene and regular professional maintenance.

How All-on-4 works

Four implants. One fixed arch. No more loose dentures.

Two implants are placed vertically at the front of the jaw where the bone is densest, and two are angled up to 45 degrees toward the back. That angle is the whole idea. It lets the implants anchor into the bone you already have, which is why most patients do not need grafting, even after years in dentures.

A fixed bridge is then secured to those implants, so your teeth are attached to your jaw rather than sitting on your gums. There is nothing to take out at night and no adhesive.

  • Fixed, not removable. Your teeth stay where they are put.
  • Usually no bone graft. The tilted design works with existing bone in most cases.
  • Teeth on the day. A temporary fixed bridge is fitted at the time of surgery for most patients.

All-on-X and complex cases

Four is the standard, not the limit.

How many implants your case needs depends on your bone quality and the forces your bite puts through them. Four is enough for many patients. Others do better on five or six, which is where the term All-on-X comes from. Your surgeon confirms the number after assessing your scan, not before.

Where the upper jaw has shrunk badly after long term denture wear, gum disease or failed implants, conventional implants may have nothing to hold onto. Rather than months of grafting, remote anchorage implants bypass the problem by anchoring in bone that does not resorb.

The finished result

A bridge made to fit your face, not just your jaw.

The temporary bridge fitted on the day of surgery carries you through healing. Over the following months the bone integrates with the implants, and once that is complete your final bridge is made and fitted, with reviews along the way.

Shade, tooth shape, lip support and the way your bite meets are all worked through at that stage, with an experienced prosthodontist or your own dentist handling the restoration alongside your surgeon.

  • Three to six months of healing between the temporary and the final bridge, in most cases.
  • Cleaned daily like natural teeth, with a few extra techniques for under the bridge that we teach you before you leave.

Every case is different, but the path from first call to final teeth follows the same four stages.

Before you enquire

Common questions

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. You are welcome to see us without one. If you do have a referral from your general dentist or GP, you may be eligible for a Medicare rebate on your consultation, and our team is happy to help you arrange one.

Am I suitable for All-on-4?

All-on-4 suits people who have lost most of their teeth, have teeth that cannot be saved, or wear a denture they find loose and uncomfortable. Because the rear implants are angled, it often works even where there has been significant bone loss. A consultation with a 3D scan gives a definitive answer either way.

Will I leave with teeth on the day of surgery?

In most full arch cases, yes. A temporary fixed bridge is attached to the implants at the time of surgery, so you go home with a full set rather than an empty mouth or a denture. The final bridge is fitted once the implants have integrated with the bone, generally three to six months later.

Do I need a bone graft?

Usually not for full arch treatment. Tilting the rear implants lets them anchor in denser bone at the front and mid jaw, which avoids grafting in most cases. Where bone volume is very limited, remote anchorage options such as zygomatic or pterygoid implants may be recommended instead. Both surgeons are trained in these techniques.

Can I be asleep for the procedure?

Yes. Depending on your case you can be treated under local anaesthetic or IV sedation in our Buderim rooms, or under a general anaesthetic in an accredited private hospital theatre with a specialist anaesthetist present.

What does it cost?

It depends on how many implants you need, whether extractions or grafting are involved, and where your surgery takes place. You receive an itemised written treatment plan after your consultation, before committing to anything. Payment plans are available, health fund rebates may apply, and for full arch treatment eligible patients may apply to the ATO for early release of super.

How long do implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular professional maintenance, implants can last for decades. We show you how to clean around them and recommend a review schedule before you leave.

I have been told there is not enough bone. Is that the end of it?

Often not. Grafting can rebuild bone volume, and for severe upper jaw bone loss, zygomatic implants anchor in the cheekbone rather than the jaw and avoid grafting altogether. Patients are referred to Dr Lincoln from across Queensland specifically for this work, so it is worth a second opinion before accepting that nothing can be done.
