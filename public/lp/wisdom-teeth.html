What a specialist brings

Lower wisdom teeth sit close to major nerves.

Impacted lower wisdom teeth sit near the inferior alveolar and lingual nerves. Assessing that anatomy on a 3D scan and managing it during surgery is precisely the work oral and maxillofacial surgeons spend years training for.

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon in Australia holds a medical degree, a dental degree, and a four year surgical fellowship. Both surgeons hold that qualification, and this is routine work for them, including the impacted and complex cases practitioners across the region refer on.

  • Imaging first. An OPG or 3D CBCT scan taken in our rooms maps root shape and nerve position before anything is booked.
  • Your individual risks explained in plain language before you consent, not a generic warning sheet.
  • Modern surgical technique, including piezoelectric surgery, to reduce post-operative swelling and discomfort.

About wisdom teeth

Not enough room in the jaw, and the problems that follow.

Most of us develop wisdom teeth, but many jaws simply do not have space for them to come through. When that happens the teeth become impacted, fully or partially, and problems follow: infected and inflamed gum over a partly erupted tooth, decay in the wisdom tooth or the molar in front of it, resorption of the neighbouring tooth, jaw pain, and occasionally cysts around a buried tooth.

Removal is also commonly recommended before or after orthodontic treatment, ahead of corrective jaw surgery, or simply as a preventive step. If your dentist has referred you, or an x-ray shows impaction, it is worth a specialist assessment sooner rather than later. Removal is generally simpler, with lower risk, in younger patients before the roots have fully formed.

Your anaesthetic options

You choose how awake you want to be

Nobody looks forward to this. That is why there are four ways to have it done, and the right one depends on your case, how anxious you are, and your budget. Whichever you choose, the surgical site is always fully numbed as well.

GA in hospital

Completely unconscious for the whole procedure, cared for by a specialist anaesthetist in an accredited private hospital theatre. You wake up with it done. The most popular choice for all four.

GA in clinic

The same fully asleep experience in our Buderim rooms with an anaesthetist present. Suitable for selected cases, without the hospital admission, and often a more affordable path to being asleep.

IV sedation

Twilight sedation delivered through a drip. You are drowsy, calm and comfortable, and most patients remember little or nothing of the procedure. A middle ground between local and a general anaesthetic.

Local anaesthetic

For simpler cases the area is fully numbed and you stay awake, in our rooms, with no fasting and no need for someone to drive you home afterwards.

Before you enquire

Common questions

Do my wisdom teeth actually need to come out?

Not always. Impacted wisdom teeth causing pain, repeated gum infection, decay or damage to the tooth in front generally should be removed, and many patients choose preventive removal before problems start. A specialist consultation with imaging gives you a straight answer either way. If they do not need to come out, we will tell you.

Can I be fully asleep for the procedure?

Yes. Most of our wisdom teeth patients choose a general anaesthetic, either in an accredited private hospital theatre or, for selected cases, in our Buderim clinic, always with an anaesthetist responsible for the anaesthetic. You are completely unconscious and wake with the procedure finished.

What is the difference between IV sedation and a general anaesthetic?

Under a general anaesthetic you are fully unconscious. Under IV sedation you are in a drowsy, twilight state: technically conscious, but deeply relaxed, and most patients remember little or nothing afterwards. Sedation can be done in our rooms, which also makes it more affordable. Your surgeon will recommend the safest option for your case.

Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed to see us, though many patients are sent by their dentist. If you have a referral from your dentist or GP you may be eligible for a Medicare rebate on the consultation. Bring any recent x-rays or an OPG along, as it may save you an imaging fee.

How long is recovery, and how much time off do I need?

Most people take two to three days off work or study and are largely back to normal within a week. Swelling peaks around day two or three and then settles. You go home with written aftercare covering pain relief, eating and keeping the sites clean, plus direct contact details for our team.

Is it painful?

You will not feel the procedure itself, whichever anaesthetic option you choose, because the area is always fully numbed as well. Afterwards expect tenderness and swelling for a few days, managed with the pain relief plan we send you home with.

What are the risks?

All surgery carries risks. For lower wisdom teeth the important ones involve the nerves that run close to the roots, which can cause temporary and rarely permanent numbness of the lip, chin or tongue. This is exactly why impacted teeth are best assessed and removed by a specialist surgeon. Your surgeon reviews your nerve anatomy on imaging and explains your individual risks before you consent to anything.

Does private health insurance cover it?

Extras cover typically contributes to the surgical fees, and hospital cover may apply to theatre and anaesthetic costs if you are treated under a general anaesthetic in hospital. Cover varies widely between funds and policies, so we give you the item numbers to check with your fund before you commit. Payment plans are also available.
