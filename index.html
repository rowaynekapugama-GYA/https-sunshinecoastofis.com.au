Common questions

Before you enquire

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. You are welcome to enquire directly and our team will arrange your consultation. Many patients are referred by their dentist or doctor, and if you have a referral or recent x-rays, bring them along.

What is the difference between an oral surgeon and an oral and maxillofacial surgeon?

In Australia, an oral and maxillofacial surgeon (OMS) is a registered specialist who has completed both a medical and a dental degree followed by a four year surgical training program and fellowship examination. Both of our surgeons hold this specialist qualification and are trained to manage the full scope of surgery of the mouth, jaws and face, including hospital based surgery under general anaesthetic.

Can I be asleep for my procedure?

Yes. Depending on your case you can be treated under local anaesthetic or IV sedation in our Buderim rooms, or under general anaesthetic in an accredited private hospital theatre with a specialist anaesthetist.

Do you see patients from Gympie, Hervey Bay and Brisbane?

Yes. Patients from the northern Sunshine Coast and Gympie can be seen at our Noosa visiting service. Hervey Bay and Fraser Coast patients can have surgery at St Stephen's Private Hospital in Urraween. Dr Lincoln also visits Rockhampton and Brisbane regularly.

What will it cost?

You receive a written, itemised treatment plan after your consultation, before you commit to anything. Payment plans are available, health fund rebates may apply, and for full arch treatment eligible patients may apply to the ATO for early release of super.

Wisdom teeth removed while you are completely asleep

Specialist oral and maxillofacial surgeons remove all four wisdom teeth in a single appointment. Choose to be fully asleep under general anaesthetic in an accredited private hospital or in our Buderim clinic, or relaxed under IV sedation.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

Why wisdom teeth cause trouble

Most of us develop wisdom teeth, but many jaws simply do not have room for them. When they cannot come through properly they become impacted, and problems follow: infected, inflamed gums over a partially erupted tooth (pericoronitis), decay in the wisdom tooth or the molar in front, resorption of the neighbouring tooth, jaw pain, and occasionally cysts around a buried tooth.

Removal is also commonly recommended before or after orthodontic treatment, ahead of corrective jaw surgery, or preventively before problems begin. If your dentist has referred you or your x-ray shows impaction, it is worth a specialist assessment sooner rather than later. Removal is generally simpler, with lower risk, in younger patients before the roots have fully formed.

Why see a specialist surgeon

Impacted lower wisdom teeth sit close to the inferior alveolar and lingual nerves. Assessing that anatomy on a 3D scan and managing it during surgery is precisely the work oral and maxillofacial surgeons train for. Both Dr Lincoln and Dr Higgins handle the complex and impacted cases that general dentists refer on, using modern techniques, including piezoelectric surgery, that reduce post-operative swelling and discomfort.

  • All four in one appointment. One procedure and one recovery, rather than returning tooth by tooth.
  • Your choice of anaesthetic. General anaesthetic in hospital or in clinic, or IV sedation, always with the surgical site fully numbed.
  • Home the same day. Day surgery in every setting, with written aftercare and direct contact with our team.

Your anaesthetic options

General anaesthetic in hospital

Completely unconscious for the whole procedure, cared for by a specialist anaesthetist in an accredited private hospital theatre. The most popular choice for all four wisdom teeth.

General anaesthetic in clinic

The same fully asleep experience in our Buderim rooms with an anaesthetist present. Suitable for selected cases, and often a more affordable path to being asleep.

IV sedation in clinic

Twilight sedation through a drip. Drowsy, calm and comfortable, and most patients remember little or nothing of the procedure.

Local anaesthetic

For simpler cases, the area is fully numbed and you remain awake, in our rooms, with no fasting or escort required.

Common questions

What patients ask us

Do my wisdom teeth actually need to come out?

Not always. Impacted wisdom teeth causing pain, repeated infection, decay or damage to the tooth in front generally should be removed, and many patients choose preventive removal. A specialist consultation with imaging gives you a straight answer either way. If they do not need to come out, we will tell you.

How long is recovery?

Most people take two to three days off and are largely back to normal within a week. Swelling peaks around day two or three then settles. You go home with written aftercare and a pain relief plan.

What are the risks?

All surgery carries risk. For lower wisdom teeth the important ones involve the nerves near the roots, which can cause temporary and rarely permanent numbness of the lip, chin or tongue. Your surgeon assesses your nerve anatomy on imaging and explains your individual risks before you consent.

Does private health insurance cover it?

Extras cover typically contributes to surgical fees, and hospital cover may apply to theatre and anaesthetic costs for a general anaesthetic in hospital. We give you item numbers so you can check your rebate before committing. Payment plans are also available.

Dental implants placed by specialist surgeons

A dental implant is a titanium fixture placed in the jaw to replace a missing tooth root, on which a crown, bridge or full arch is secured. Planned on 3D imaging and placed by a specialist oral and maxillofacial surgeon, then restored by your dentist or prosthodontist.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

A fixed, long-term replacement for missing teeth

Implants restore the ability to eat, speak and smile with confidence, and they preserve the jaw bone that would otherwise shrink after a tooth is lost. Unlike a bridge, an implant does not rely on grinding down neighbouring teeth, and unlike a denture, it does not move.

We place single implants, multiple implants for bridges, and implants to secure loose dentures, as well as full arch solutions. Where a tooth is failing, it can often be removed and the implant placed at the same visit (an immediate implant), shortening treatment time.

Why implant surgery with a specialist

  • Surgical training. Oral and maxillofacial surgeons complete medicine, dentistry and a four year surgical fellowship. Implant placement, grafting and management of nerves and sinuses are core specialist skills.
  • 3D planning. Every implant is planned on a CBCT scan taken in our rooms, so position, angle and bone volume are known before surgery starts.
  • Complex and compromised cases. We routinely treat patients with bone loss, sinus proximity, medical conditions or previous failed implants, in clinic under sedation or in hospital under general anaesthetic.
  • Team approach. Your dentist or prosthodontist restores the final crown or bridge, and we coordinate the plan with them from the start.

Bone grafting and implants

If a tooth has been missing for some time there may not be enough bone to hold an implant securely. Options include socket preservation at the time of extraction, sinus lifts for the upper back jaw, and block or particulate grafting. See jaw bone grafting. For patients with severe upper jaw bone loss, zygomatic implants can avoid grafting altogether.

Am I suitable for a dental implant?

Most adults with good general health and adequate bone are suitable. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and some medications affect healing, and bone loss may need grafting first. The consultation and 3D scan give a definitive answer.

Is the procedure painful?

How long do implants last?

With good oral hygiene and regular maintenance, implants can last for decades. We will show you how to clean around them and recommend a review schedule.

Do you do the crown as well?

The final crown or bridge is made by your dentist or prosthodontist. If you do not have one, we can recommend an experienced restorative clinician on the Coast.

Failing or missing teeth, replaced with a fixed full arch in a single day

All-on-4 replaces every tooth in the upper or lower jaw on four or more titanium implants. No loose dentures, no adhesives, and in most cases no bone grafting. Payment plans are available, and eligible patients may be able to use early release of super.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

Four implants. One fixed arch. No more loose dentures.

Two implants are placed vertically at the front of the jaw where bone is densest, and two are angled up to 45 degrees toward the back. That angle lets the implants anchor in the bone you already have, which is why most patients do not need grafting, even after years in dentures. A fixed bridge is attached to those implants so your new teeth are secured to your jaw rather than resting on your gums.

Some cases need more than four implants for stability, which is what the term All-on-X refers to. Your surgeon confirms exactly what your case needs after a 3D scan.

  • Fixed, not removable. Your teeth stay in place, with no adhesives and no taking them out at night.
  • Usually no bone graft. The tilted implant design works with existing bone in most cases.
  • A temporary bridge on the day. Most patients leave surgery with a full set of teeth.
  • Surgeon led, start to finish. Diagnosis, surgery and follow-up under one specialist team, with the final bridge made by an experienced prosthodontist or dentist.

Why full arch surgery with a specialist

Full arch rehabilitation is Dr Lincoln's particular focus. He undertook advanced training in zygomatic implants in Hong Kong and zygomatic, nasal and pterygoid implants in Texas, and has trained further with leading surgeons from France, Brazil and Spain. Dr Higgins has a particular interest in complex implant surgery, grafting and full arch restoration. Where bone volume is very limited, both surgeons offer remote anchorage options such as zygomatic implants, so very few patients are turned away.

Fixed teeth when you have been told there is not enough bone

Zygomatic implants are longer implants anchored in the dense cheekbone (zygoma) rather than the upper jaw. For patients with severe upper jaw bone loss they provide a fixed full arch, often on the same day, without months of bone grafting.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

Remote anchorage for the atrophic upper jaw

When the upper jaw has shrunk after long-term denture wear, gum disease or failed implants, conventional implants may have nothing to hold onto. The traditional answer was extensive grafting: bone taken from the hip or skull, months of healing, and no guarantee of success. Remote anchorage implants bypass the problem by anchoring in bone that does not resorb.

  • Zygomatic implants anchor in the cheekbone, supporting the back of a full arch bridge.
  • Pterygoid implants engage the dense bone behind the upper jaw.
  • Nasal and trans-sinus implants use the bone beside the nasal cavity and sinus floor at the front.

Combined with conventional or All-on-4 style implants where bone allows, these techniques let both surgeons offer a fixed solution to patients who have previously been told nothing can be done.

Specialist training you can check

Zygomatic surgery is advanced, technique-sensitive work performed by a small number of surgeons in Australia. Dr Lincoln trained in zygomatic implants in Hong Kong in 2017 and in zygomatic, nasal and pterygoid implants in Texas in 2023, and has since trained with leading surgeons from France, Brazil and Spain. Patients are referred to the practice from across Queensland for this procedure.

How it is done

Zygomatic surgery is performed under general anaesthetic in an accredited private hospital, with a specialist anaesthetist. The implants are planned on 3D CBCT imaging and in many cases a temporary fixed bridge is attached on the day of surgery, so you go home with teeth. The final bridge follows once healing is complete.

Rebuilding bone so implants have something to hold

After a tooth is lost the surrounding bone shrinks. Bone grafting restores the height and width of the jaw so implants can be placed in the right position and last. Performed by specialist surgeons in our rooms or in hospital.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

Types of grafting we perform

Socket preservation

A graft placed into the socket at the time of extraction to prevent the bone collapsing, keeping the site ready for an implant a few months later.

Sinus lift

In the upper back jaw the sinus sits close to the teeth. A sinus lift gently raises the sinus membrane and adds bone beneath it so implants can be placed.

Ridge augmentation

Particulate or block grafts, sometimes with membranes or titanium mesh, to widen or heighten a thin ridge before implant placement.

Soft tissue grafting

Gum grafting around implants and teeth to improve health, stability and appearance.

Graft materials

Depending on the site and the volume required, grafts may use your own bone (taken from elsewhere in the jaw), processed donor bone, bovine-derived bone or synthetic materials, often combined with growth factors from your own blood. Your surgeon will explain the options and recommend the material best suited to your case.

Do I actually need a graft?

Not always. Modern implant techniques, including tilted implants for full arch cases and remote anchorage zygomatic implants, avoid grafting for many patients. The consultation and 3D scan determine whether grafting is genuinely necessary, and if it is, whether it can be done at the same time as implant placement to shorten your treatment.

Common questions

What patients ask us

Is bone grafting painful?

Grafting is done under local anaesthetic, sedation or general anaesthetic. Afterwards, swelling and discomfort are similar to a tooth extraction and settle within a week or so.

How long before I can have implants?

Small grafts and socket preservation usually heal in three to four months. Larger sinus lifts and block grafts may take six to nine months.

What are the risks?

Infection, graft loss and, for sinus lifts, sinus membrane perforation are the main risks. Both are uncommon in the hands of specialist surgeons and are discussed in detail before you consent.

Can grafting be avoided?

Often, yes. Tilted implants, short implants and zygomatic implants are alternatives your surgeon will consider first.

Corrective jaw surgery to align your bite and your face

Orthognathic surgery repositions the upper jaw, lower jaw or both to correct a bite that braces alone cannot fix. It improves chewing, speech and breathing, and brings the face into balance. Planned in partnership with your orthodontist and performed in hospital.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

When jaw surgery is recommended

Some bite problems are caused by the position of the teeth, and orthodontics alone corrects them. Others are caused by the size or position of the jaws themselves: a lower jaw that sits too far back or forward, an upper jaw that is too narrow or too long, an open bite where the front teeth do not meet, or facial asymmetry. In those cases braces move the teeth into position and surgery moves the jaws.

  • Difficulty biting, chewing or speaking
  • Receding or protruding chin, gummy smile or long face
  • Open bite, deep bite or crossbite that cannot be corrected orthodontically
  • Facial asymmetry
  • Obstructive sleep apnoea related to jaw position
  • Jaw joint (TMJ) pain related to bite

A team approach

Orthognathic treatment is a partnership between you, your orthodontist and your surgeon. Braces or aligners align the teeth before surgery (usually 12 to 18 months), the surgery repositions the jaws, and a shorter period of orthodontics after surgery fine-tunes the bite. Your surgeon uses 3D imaging and digital planning so the movements are precise and predictable, and both surgeons trained in orthognathic surgery across Queensland's major teaching hospitals.

The surgery

Surgery is performed inside the mouth under general anaesthetic in an accredited private hospital, so there are no visible facial scars. The jaws are repositioned and held with small titanium plates and screws that stay in place permanently. Most patients stay in hospital for one to two nights and return to school or work in two to three weeks, with swelling settling over the following weeks and months.

Repair and reconstruction of facial injuries

Fractured jaws, cheekbones, eye sockets and noses, knocked out teeth and facial lacerations from sport, falls, accidents and assaults. Oral and maxillofacial surgeons are the specialists trained to restore both function and appearance after facial trauma.

Make an enquiry(07) 5406 0586

Injuries we treat

  • Fractures of the lower jaw (mandible) and upper jaw (maxilla)
  • Cheekbone (zygomatic) and eye socket (orbital) fractures
  • Nasal fractures and injuries to the nose and sinuses
  • Dentoalveolar trauma: knocked out, displaced or broken teeth and the bone around them
  • Facial lacerations and soft tissue injuries
  • Delayed or previously treated injuries that have not healed as expected

Why a maxillofacial surgeon

The face combines bone, teeth, nerves, eyes, airways and skin in a small space. Restoring the bite, protecting the nerves, preserving vision and achieving a good cosmetic result all at once is the core training of oral and maxillofacial surgery. Dr Lincoln discovered the specialty treating complex facial trauma at the Princess Alexandra Hospital and has published on paediatric orbital fractures and return-to-play protocols after facial injury. Dr Higgins trained in facial trauma across Brisbane's major hospitals and Royal Darwin.

Sports injuries

Football, rugby, surfing, cycling and other sports account for many of the facial fractures we see on the Coast. Timely assessment matters: fractures are best repaired within the first one to two weeks, and knocked out teeth have the best chance when re-implanted quickly. We provide clear return-to-play advice so you can get back to sport safely.

Treatment

Minor injuries can be managed in our Buderim rooms. Fractures requiring surgery are repaired under general anaesthetic in a private hospital, usually through incisions inside the mouth or hidden in natural skin creases, with small titanium plates holding the bone while it heals. Most patients are home within a day or two.
